When the lanterns go out

A small light can outlast the rain.

The town had learned to be quiet after dark.

By the time Hana reached the bridge, the last shop on the river had turned its lights off. The paper lanterns above the street were still glowing, but their colour had begun to fade into the rain.

She had come to find the woman in the blue jacket, the friend Ren wrote beside the ending of every unfinished letter. Someone had her grandmother’s handwriting in the pocket of a coat.

“You were not meant to read that one,” he said. He sounded less like someone guarding a secret than someone who had spent years trying to give it back.
