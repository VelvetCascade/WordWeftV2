# WordWeft Classics worldwide rights evidence

Review date: 27 November 2026. Target: an India-based publisher offering English-language editions globally, with the option to charge later. This record documents a conservative editorial screen for original historical text. It is not a legal opinion or a worldwide release approval.

## What qualifies for this catalog

- An English-language print record for a named literary author, with an Internet Archive catalog date no later than 1915 and an author authority entry reporting death in or before 1920. The period is a deliberate buffer against long ordinary copyright terms. A catalog date can be approximate, inherited from a copyright notice, or wrong. The exact date printed on the copy controls the edition-evidence step.
- The import suggestion is the historical **body text**. Don’t copy a current publisher’s transcription, introduction, cover, editorial annotations, corrected text, translation, scan images, or modern layout. Use the historical printed pages as evidence for a WordWeft transcription, subject to checking provenance and access/reuse conditions. Gutenberg links are discovery references only. No e-book IDs are invented for archive-only entries.
- Known higher-risk cases such as *Peter Pan*, Oz, Tarzan, and later franchise-derived material were screened out of this pass. An exclusion is a prioritization decision, not a legal judgment about the original work. Less familiar archival rows have less editorial evidence and remain in a separate evidence group.
- This is a body-text catalog only. Artwork, branding and every later contribution get separate records. Unknown translator, anonymous adaptation, modern foreword, partial edition, or uncertain print date means hold the item or find a better witness.

## What gets approved for a global paid release

1. **Establish the edition.** Inspect title page, imprint and colophon. Distinguish publication date from copyright date. Check every volume and chapter from the first page to the final page. Note where body text starts and ends. Record stable item IDs and a checksum of the resulting clean text. Do not infer completeness from an archive identifier.
2. **Establish the contributors.** Confirm authority records and name variants. Identify coauthors, translators, editors, illustrators and later interpolations. Keep a separate check for each component actually distributed. Quotes and editorial notes should be original to WordWeft or independently cleared.
3. **Review the whole distribution plan.** Document commercial text rights, applicable local exceptions, source terms, and any separate rights over scans or format. A record's digitization date, access flag, or U.S. public-domain label is not evidence of worldwide permission. The plan assumes worldwide access. Hold a work if a material rights problem remains unresolved rather than silently limiting the audience.
4. **Preserve attribution and text integrity.** Show author and actual edition credits. Visually distinguish editorial additions and maintain the original prose and verse. Label adaptations accurately. Never imply endorsement by a publisher, estate or adaptation studio. Historical author records are editorial attribution entries with a bibliography and no Follow, Message, or sign-in.
5. **Clear the release brand and assets.** Use WordWeft-owned or properly licensed assets with documented worldwide commercial web and paid-social rights. Search the product/series brand, edition presentation and ads, including relevant national registers. A database search cannot rule out unregistered marks, passing off, or conflicts in every country. Avoid adaptation imagery and partner seals. [WIPO Global Brand Database](https://branddb.wipo.int/) · [IP India trade mark search](https://tmrsearch.ipindia.gov.in/tmrpublicsearch/) · [Project Gutenberg license](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/license.html)
6. **Record the decision before release.** Fill in the [rights register](rights-review-template.csv) for exact body text, artwork and marketing assets. Record sources, reviewer and decision. Until that record supports worldwide release of every distributed component, keep the release hold. The same rights gate applies to free samples and paid marketing. Charging later does not change whether an underlying public-domain text can be sold.

WordWeft can sell access to its edition, editorial features and reading experience after the material is cleared. Avoid claiming exclusive rights in historical body text that anyone can publish.
