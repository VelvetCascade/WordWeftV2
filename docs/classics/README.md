# WordWeft Classics

[Browse the 1,000-work catalog](catalog.csv). Every row includes a candidate edition, a demand signal, an editorial reason to include it, a suggested WordWeft treatment, and a release hold. The catalog contains metadata and original editorial suggestions only.

## A launch shelf worth editing by hand

| Work | What to build into the edition |
| --- | --- |
| Pride and Prejudice | Distinguish Darcy’s letter from surrounding prose and reveal character relationships as they appear. |
| Dracula | Make the diary, letter, and newspaper changes legible. Test optional atmosphere on the way to the castle. |
| The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes | Give each case its own entry. Place the solution and optional clue recap behind the case boundary. |
| Frankenstein | Make the nested narrators and Walton’s letters easy to follow. Check the actual edition before choosing between historical versions. |
| Alice’s Adventures in Wonderland | Preserve verse and speaker transitions. Clear any illustration separately. |
| A Christmas Carol | Navigate by stave. Give readers an optional transition between the visits, with reduced motion. |
| The Count of Monte Cristo | Make aliases useful without revealing their links early. Compare available English translations before deciding on a text. |
| Moby-Dick | Keep quotations and headings intact. Use a small optional glossary for whaling terms. |

Start with these eight once cleared. For the 1,000-work backlist, preserve each source’s original structure first. The launch shortlist has work-specific notes. Most other treatments are proposals based on catalog subjects, title, and form and need editorial review before implementation. Use tables for actual tabular content, scene formatting for plays, stanza formatting for poetry, and quotations where the text actually quotes. Keep optional notes and discussions chapter-aware. Test hiding spoilers across search excerpts, share cards, notifications, and progress reset. Offer no-motion and high-contrast reading.

Represent historical authors as editorial records, separate from user accounts. An author page can show a bibliography and an attribution statement, with no Follow, Message, or sign-in identity. Use no estate seal, publisher mark, or adaptation imagery.

## Rights gate before import or advertising

**All rows are on hold.** Gutendex indexes Project Gutenberg records. Its U.S. public-domain flag is a preliminary filter, not commercial clearance. The linked e-book ID points to a candidate transcription, not proof of a particular edition’s underlying rights. Where available, downloads are the source catalog’s reported counts, a relative interest signal, not worldwide fame, unique readers, or forecasts. Rankings put 20 selected editorial candidates first, then use the reported count where available. The remaining entries come from the metadata mirror in numeric e-book ID order, with no measured recognition claimed. Title/author normalization removes obvious repeated editions. Collections can share individual stories with standalone works. Verify contents before importing both.

1. Record the exact original edition, publication date and place, language, translator, editor, foreword, notes, artwork credits, source URL, and a checksum for the text actually imported. Keep this evidence in the release register, with a reviewer and review date.
2. Clear each element in every launch territory. For ordinary U.S. published books, publication through 1930 is a useful 2026 starting screen. Verify publication facts and exceptions. Other countries apply different terms and rules. Confirm territory controls before any geographically limited release, including excerpts, downloads, and marketing access.
3. Use only separately cleared historical translations, illustrations, maps, introductions, and supplemental text, or commission replacements with written commercial rights. A public-domain story does not automatically clear later additions. If contributor or edition facts are unclear, keep the hold.
4. Check the source’s redistribution terms. Review Project Gutenberg’s license and trademark provisions before using a Gutenberg copy in a paid product or campaign. Keep provenance internally. Avoid Gutenberg branding in the product unless its terms are satisfied. Prefer WordWeft-owned cover art from original briefs with documented rights. Screen edition titles and ad copy for branding or endorsement confusion and review special cases individually, including UK rules for *Peter Pan*.
5. Charge for WordWeft’s edition and reading experience. Describe the source accurately and avoid implying exclusive rights to the underlying public-domain text. Obtain legal review for launch territories and written approval per exact edition before ingest, release, or paid promotion.
