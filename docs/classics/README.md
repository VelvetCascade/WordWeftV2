# WordWeft Classics · worldwide candidate catalog

[Browse or download the 1,000-work catalog](catalog.csv) · [Worldwide rights evidence and release rules](worldwide-rights.md) · [Per-edition rights register template](rights-review-template.csv).

This replaces the discovery list with historical edition evidence. Each row links a dated print record and gives its publisher, reported author death year, popularity signal, selection reason and possible WordWeft treatment. The target is an India-based edition for worldwide readers, with optional monetization later. Every row carries a worldwide publish hold pending clearance of the actual text and all elements shipped with it.

Verify the full set and transcribe the text in order. | Introduce relationships at their first appearance. Keep the Thornfield reveal out of public preview metadata. |

A source can have a correct work title and misleading edition date. A Modern Library printing of *Dracula* was catalogued under 1897, but the OCR shows that 1897 is a copyright notice. It was removed from this edition selection. The first volumes of *Jane Eyre* and *Great Expectations* now link to the rest of their matched sets in the CSV. These checks identify candidates for edition work while respecting the release hold.

For the wider catalog, suggestions use the available form and subject evidence. Use chapter-aware story-bible notes where relationships or lore benefit from them, and let readers opt out of atmosphere. Preserve play scenes, stanza breaks, verse indentation, source quotations and existing chapter divisions. Use tables only when the printed source is tabular. Keep private notes and progress intact. Spoiler controls need to work across the reader, search snippets, sharing and notifications. Historical author pages are editorial bibliographies, with no account, Follow or Message actions.

## Reading the evidence

- **Archive item record checked** means the individual archive metadata was retrieved and checked for date, access and volume indicators. **Archive search record only** means the dates and creator detail come from the archive search record. Neither designation certifies that the whole scan is complete or that all contributors have been identified.
- Rows with a Gutenberg match have extra discovery metadata. Rows without a match are archive-only candidates tied to named authors in the historical literary pool. Check description and literary fit before selecting a less familiar archive-only entry. Unique archive item IDs and obvious duplicate titles were checked. Collections and standalone works can still share material.
- The archive download count is for the selected scan. The Gutenberg count, when present, refers to a different digital record for the work. These are signals of interest in those records. The ordering prioritizes stronger evidence and title-specific launch notes before download signals.
- The [validation summary](catalog-validation.json) gives the evidence counts. This catalog contains metadata and editorial suggestions. It does not distribute works.
